# Map of Indian Philosophy — the chronological spine

A single navigational spine across all 62 episodes. The series is roughly
chronological by tradition, but jumps around; this map reorders the whole India
series by **period and school**, so you can see how the ideas develop and find the
episode(s) that cover each. `(Ep N)` = the episode(s) on that development.

> Built from the series itself — a study aid, not a substitute for the episodes.

## Deep background — the Vedic world (c. 1500–600 BCE)
- **Before the Aryans:** the Indus Valley cities of Harappa and Mohenjo-daro `(Ep 3)`.
- **The Aryans, the horse, and the Vedas:** the horse sacrifice, the four classes, the *Puruṣa* hymn `(Ep 3)`.
- **The orientation:** what "philosophy in India" even means, and "beginning at the end" with the highest good `(Ep 1)`; a whirlwind historical overview of everything to come `(Ep 2)`.

## The Upaniṣads and the first philosophy (c. 800–400 BCE)
- **The Upaniṣads:** the self (*ātman*) and ultimate reality (*brahman*), "hide and seek" `(Ep 4)`; Indra's search for the self `(Ep 5)`; an interview on reading them `(Ep 7, Brian Black)`.
- **Karma and rebirth** as the engine of the whole tradition `(Ep 6)`.
- **Pāṇini** and the science of grammar `(Ep 8)`.

## The heterodox challenge — Buddhists and Jains (5th c. BCE onward)
- **The Buddha** and the nature of his teaching: suffering, no-self, the middle way `(Ep 9, 10)`; an interview on Buddhism and the self `(Ep 12, Rupert Gethin)`.
- **Political thought** and the Mauryan order — Kauṭilya's *Arthaśāstra* `(Ep 11)`.

## The epics and ethics (c. 400 BCE–400 CE)
- **The Mahābhārata:** *dharma* and deception `(Ep 13)`; the **Bhagavad-Gītā** `(Ep 14)`.
- **Non-violence (*ahiṃsā*)** `(Ep 15)`; **women in ancient India** `(Ep 16)`; an interview on Hinduism and philosophy `(Ep 17, Jessica Frazier)`.

## The age of the sūtra — the six systems form (c. 200 BCE–500 CE)
- **The sūtra style and the rise of skepticism** `(Ep 18, 19)`.
- **Mīmāṃsā** — ritual, knowledge, and language: Jaimini `(Ep 20)`, knowledge & language `(Ep 21)`, interview `(Ep 22, Elisa Freschi)`.
- **Vedānta** — Bādarāyaṇa's *Vedānta-Sūtra* `(Ep 23)`, Śaṅkara and Advaita `(Ep 24)`, interview `(Ep 26, Francis Clooney)`; **Bhartṛhari** on language `(Ep 25)`.
- **Sāṃkhya** — Īśvarakṛṣṇa's evolution of *prakṛti* `(Ep 27)`; early **Āyurvedic medicine** `(Ep 28)`.
- **Yoga** — Patañjali's *Yoga-Sūtra* `(Ep 29)`, interview `(Ep 30, Philipp Maas)`.
- **Nyāya** — logic and epistemology: Gautama's *Nyāya-Sūtra* `(Ep 31)`, perception `(Ep 32)`, reasoning `(Ep 33)`, the mind & liberation `(Ep 34)`, interview `(Ep 35, Ujjwala Jha & V.N. Jha)`.
- **Vaiśeṣika** — atoms and categories: Kaṇāda's *Sūtra* `(Ep 36)`, wholes & causation `(Ep 37)`; **theories of time** `(Ep 38)`.
- **Naturalism and materialism** — Indian naturalism `(Ep 39)`, materialist (Cārvāka) theories of the self `(Ep 40)`, interview on philosophy of mind `(Ep 41, Monima Chadha)`.
- **Aesthetics** — the theory of *rasa* `(Ep 42)`.

## The great Buddhist and Jain philosophers (c. 150–600 CE)
- **Overview:** the Buddhists and Jains as systematic philosophers `(Ep 43)`.
- **Nāgārjuna and Madhyamaka** — emptiness `(Ep 44)`, change/motion `(Ep 45)`, the tetralemma `(Ep 46)`, interview `(Ep 47, Jan Westerhoff)`.
- **Jain epistemology** — the theory of standpoints `(Ep 48)`, the Jains on truth `(Ep 49)`, interview `(Ep 50, Marie-Hélène Gorisse)`.
- **Yogācāra** — Vasubandhu and "consciousness-only" `(Ep 51)`.
- **Dignāga and Buddhist epistemology** — perception & language `(Ep 52)`, logic `(Ep 53)`, consciousness `(Ep 55)`; interview on logic & Buddhism `(Ep 54, Graham Priest)`.
- **Buddhaghosa** and Theravāda `(Ep 56)`; **Tantra** `(Ep 57)`.
